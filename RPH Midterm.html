4 Strategies for Intrusion and Conquest

  • 1
    Policy of Attraction and Deception
    Misinterpretation of Filipino hospitality · sangduguan → pacto de sangre
  • 2
    Entrada
    All expeditions were military expeditions
  • 3
    Catolicismo
    Jives well with fundamentals of animism · instilling fear through "infierno" · "en cada fraile tenía el rey de España en Filipinas un capitán y un ejercito entero"
  • 4
    Divide et impera
    Took advantage of geographical boundaries and existing feuds between barangays

Administrative Policies

Encomienda and Reducción
Church-plaza complex · bajo del son de las campanas · caused internal and external metamorphosis of precolonial barangays

Colonial Administration — Check-and-Balance

Residencia
A formal review/audit of outgoing colonial officials before they could leave office.
Visita
Surprise inspections conducted on colonial officials while still in office.
Frailocracia / La Soberanía Monacál: The amalgamation (union) of Church and State — amalgamated but disunited. The friars held enormous power; described as "each friar was a captain and an entire army of the King of Spain."

Economic Institutions

Encomienda
≠ land grant. An administrative role entrusted to a conquistador. Evolved into two kinds: Encomienda de la real corona (crown) and Encomienda de particulares (private).

Taxation Without Representation

  • Tributo — income tax, customs duties
  • Bandalas / Compras reales — forced purchase of goods
  • Rentas estancadas — state monopolies on key goods
  • Samboangan / Donativo de Zamboanga — vinta, falua
  • Cedula personal — personal identification tax (replaced tributo in 1884)
Polo y Servicios
Prestación personal — forced labor. Polistas: 16–60 years old. Net effect: upset the village economy.

Manila-Acapulco Galleon Trade

Galleon de Manila vis-à-vis Nao de China · neglected native industries and arrested population growth · allowed intercultural exchange
Real Sociedad Economica de Amigos del País (1780–1805)
Despotismo ilustrado · Plán Generál Economicó
Real Compania de Filipinas (1785–1814)
Sought to unite American and Asian commerce to compete with the Manila-Acapulco Galleon Trade

Legacies of Spanish Colonization

CategoryLegacies
PoliticalCentralized government · unitary ≠ unified · highly-politicized Church · "weak state" · politics of patronage · leader → dealer
EconomicEncomienda as nucleus of provinces · trope of retrogression · stunted potential developments · change in notion of "property"
Socio-culturalPartial hispanization · bahay kubo → casa mestiza · baro't saya → vestida mestiza · cementerio · Fiesta · compadrazgo · theocentric literature and arts · appropriated language

Lasting Impacts of Spanish Colonial Experience

  • Deep religiosity → false religiosity, dogmatism
  • Racial discrimination
  • Cultural and religious divide between Christians and others
  • Social climbing
  • Destroyed: valuing the past
  • Colonial mentality
  • Failure to come up with own national language
All these lead to the bases of Filipinism and Nationalism.
Topic 2 — 1700s–1800s

From Indio to Filipino: Filipino Nationalism

Local Revolts — Why They Failed

Causes: Resistance to Spanish-imposed institutions · Religious Motives · Personal Motives
Result: FAILURE
Why? Absence of NATIONALISM

Conceptualizing Nationalism

Popular Dichotomies

reforma vs. revolución

patriotismo vs. Nacionalismo

Definition of Nationalism

Belongingness to a NACIÓN. A nation is an imagined community — people who will never know each other but share alive in their minds the image of their communion.

Filipino Nationalism = metamorphosis from INDIO to FILIPINO
Crucial requisite: Development of lingua franca and printing

19th Century Context — 3 Milieus

Political Milieu: Administrative Reforms
  • 1760s: Fall of Manila to the British
  • Political Reforms: Real Situado · Representación a la Camara Española · Presa libre · Secularización de las parroquias
  • 1861: Real Audiencia's loose administration → more power to alcaldes mayores (indulto de comercio)
  • 1866: Alcalde mayor as judge of first instance
  • 1869: Establishment of Guardia Civil; Bilibid at Fort Santiago for crimes against the state
  • 1884: Tribute replaced with cedula personal — "attestation of tributary slavery to Madre España"
  • 1887: Social activities declared crimes against the state
Economic Milieu: Development of Export Economy
  • First Industrial Revolution · Consolidation of Church wealth
  • Abolition of trade barriers (Galleon Trade, tobacco monopoly)
  • Cash crops: sugar cane · coffee bean · tobacco · hemp · indigo
  • Introduction of: daang bakal (railroad), bapor (steamship), telegrapo (telegraph)
  • Comprador = Chinese middlemen who buy crops and sell to foreign agents · Tokay system
  • Effects: Inflated local economy · Proliferation of haciendas · Conversion of rice fields to cash crop fields
Socio-cultural Milieu: Opportunity to Intellectualize
  • Educational Decrees: opening of primary education
  • Return of the Jesuits: Escuela Normal de Maestros
  • Conversion of Escuela Pia to Ateneo Municipal de Manila — humanistic and "modern" education

Road to Nationalism: Key Events

1860s: Comité de Reformadores
Mandate: to guard reforms and uphold status of Philippines as a "province" of Spain. Demanded: Real Situado · Representación · Codigo Penal · Presa libre · Secularización. Members: secular priests (GomBurZa), laity (Maximo Paterno), students (Juventud Escolar Liberal — Gregorio Sanciangco, Paciano Mercado)
January 1872: La Algarada Caviteña (Cavite Mutiny)
Not an ordinary revolt — planned by the Comité de Reformadores, a proto-national movement. Despite premature nature, still an attempt to overturn colonial rule.
February 17, 1872: GomBurZa Execution
Identified as the beginning of Filipino nationalism. Actors: secular priests. Place: Bagumbayan. Rizal connection: Paciano Mercado (Rizal's brother). Led to the Comité de Propaganda.
1872: Comité de Propaganda
Objectives: dishearten reforms idea · pursue Fr. Jose Burgos' advocacy for separation · strengthen financial base · gather ideas for the Philippines' future. By 1882: full-blown — sent scholars abroad to the European front.
La Liga Filipina (1892)
Fines (Goals): Unite the archipelago into a compact, vigorous, homogeneous body · Mutual protection in all want and necessity · Defense against violence and injustice · Encourage education, agriculture, industry, and commerce · Study and application of reforms
Katipunan / KKK (1892)
Full name: Kataastaasan Kagalang-galangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan. Objectives: Political (separation from Spain) · Moral (good manners, attacking fanaticism) · Civic (self-help, defense of the poor). Membership: Katipon (Anak ng Bayan) · Kawal (GomBurZa) · Bayani (Rizal). Structure: Sangguniang Bayan · Sangguniang Balangay · Sangguniang Hukuman. Publications: Kartilya · "Kalayaan" · Katungkulang gagawin ng mga anak ng bayan

Source Classification (10 items) — Primary or Secondary? Who / What?

Classify: "Travels in the Philippines" by Feodor Jagor
Primary Source — a first-hand account by a scientist-traveller who personally travelled and observed the Philippines during the colonial period
Classify: "Sobre la Indolencia de los Filipinos" published in La Solidaridad
Primary Source — landmark essay written by Rizal in the context of the Propaganda Movement, arguing against the Spanish-imposed narrative of Filipino laziness
Classify: "Filipinas dentro de cien años" published in La Solidaridad
Primary Source — another landmark essay by Rizal predicting the Philippines' future in 100 years; published during the Propaganda Movement
Classify: Kartilya ng Katipunan
Primary Source — the KKK's primary publication containing the moral and civic code of members; a direct artifact of the revolutionary organization
Classify: "Revista del Circulo Hispano Filipino"
Primary Source — published by the Circulo Hispano-Filipino (1882, Madrid); a direct publication of Filipino students abroad during the Propaganda period
Classify: "El Progreso de Filipinas" by Gregorio Sanciangco
Primary Source — directly written by a Filipino propagandist; discussed with Rizal and the Paternos in the Circulo Hispano-Filipino setting
Classify: Acta de Tejeros
Primary Source — drafted by Bonifacio himself to nullify the results of the Tejeros Convention; a direct historical document
Classify: Rizal's 1890 letter/statement — "Now, I tell you. I have lost my hope in Spain…"
Primary Source — Rizal's personal statement declaring his shift from reformist to revolutionary stance; a direct quote from a key historical figure
Classify: "Nosotros Filipinos hemos aspirado..." (Sol's Reformas section)
Primary Source — the introductory line of La Solidaridad's reform agenda; a direct primary source of the Propaganda Movement's programmatic objectives
Classify: A modern textbook summarizing the GomBurZa execution
Secondary Source — a later account written by a historian analyzing and summarizing historical events from GomBurZa's time, not a direct participant account
