Key Concepts in Globalization

What is Globalization?

Core Idea: A satisfactory definition of globalization must address EXTENSION, INTENSITY, CELERITY (speed), and IMPACT.

Understanding of the world and the increased perception of the world as a whole.

Rapid global interconnection, deep and on large scale — requires complex research.

✅ Proponents Say

Globalization is desirable and necessary for human society. Measured by:

  • Infrastructure
  • Tourist destination
❌ Critics Say

Globalization is detrimental to social welfare. Concerns include:

  • Technology dependence
  • Data privacy
  • Digital era issues

Wallerstein's World-System Theory Understand

Core
Most Developed
Dominate & exploit periphery for labor & raw materials. Powerful, high-skill, industrialized.
Examples: Western Europe, North America, Japan
Semi-Periphery
Middle Ground
Less dependent on core. Intermediate economic position with more diversified economies.
Examples: India, Brazil
Periphery
Least Developed
Dependent on core for capital. Low-skill, labor-intensive, historically colonized.
Examples: Parts of Africa, Asia, Middle East
Result: Global Inequality — peripheral countries become more dependent on core/central economies.

Cultural Concepts

Assimilation
We adapt to a new culture. The original culture is replaced.
Acculturation
The original culture is maintained even when adopting a new culture. Both are preserved.

3 Impacts of Globalization Memorize

  • 1
    Delocalization and Over-territoriality
    Economic activities are global; work can be done in real-time on a planetary scale from a distance.
  • 2
    Decline of Power of National Governments
    Policies are market-driven. Governments must adapt to transnational market forces to survive.
  • 3
    Emergence of Global Brands and Institutions
    Multinationals affect communities worldwide, exploiting cheapest labor and resources. Examples: Coca-Cola, Nike.
📌 Key Fact: The "brand" shift is exemplified by corporations like Coca-Cola and Nike that have become part of life for a very large number of people worldwide.

Structuring the Globalization

United Nations & MDGs

🇺🇳
The UN & Millennium Development Goals
  • The UN created 8 Millennium Development Goals (MDGs) in 2000
  • UN (2015): 836 million people still live in extreme poverty (down from 1.9 billion)
  • World Bank predicted: by 2030, extreme poverty could drop to under 400 million

Extreme Poverty

Philippine Poverty Threshold: Living on ₱275/day or ₱100,534/year

UN Definition of Extreme Poverty (2015): Severe deprivation of basic human needs including food, safe drinking water, sanitation, health, shelter, education, and information.

Why is Extreme Poverty Falling?

  • 1Better access to education
  • 2Humanitarian aid
  • 3Policies of international organizations like the UN
  • 4Economic Globalization — the greatest contributor

Economic Globalization

Internalization
Extension of economic activities of nation-states across borders.
Externalization
Functional integration between internationally dispersed activities.
Dickens (2004): "Economic globalization is rather a qualitative transformation than just a quantitative change."
Boyer & Drache (1996): Globalization redefined the role of the nation-state as an effective manager of the national economy. New actors appear — the major players are Transnational Corporations (TNCs), which are constantly evolving.

4 Dimensions of Economic Globalization Memorize

  • aGlobalization of trade of goods and services
  • bGlobalization of financial and capital markets
  • cGlobalization of technology and communication
  • dGlobalization of production

Type 1: Protectionism Understand

Definition: A policy of systematic government intervention in foreign trade to encourage domestic production by giving preferential treatment to domestic producers. (McAleese, 2007)

4 Types of Protectionism

  • 1
    Tariffs
    Taxes/duties on imports that increase their price in the domestic market, reducing demand. Example: "Chicken Tax" — 25% tariff on certain imported vehicles imposed by President Lyndon Johnson in 1963, still in effect.
  • 2
    Quotas
    Restrictions on the volume of imports for a good/service over a period. Known as "non-tariff trade barrier."
  • 3
    Subsidies
    Negative taxes/tax credits given by the government to domestic producers. Creates a price discrepancy between consumers and producers.
  • 4
    Standardization
    Government may require foreign products to meet certain guidelines, tending to reduce foreign products in the market.

Type 2: Trade Liberalization / Free Trade

Policy between two or more nations permitting unlimited import/export. Eliminates tariffs and makes corporations more competitive.

  • 1948GATT — General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade. U.S. + 100+ countries agreed to reduce tariffs and trade barriers.
  • 1995GATT replaced by WTO (World Trade Organization). Today: 164 countries = 98% of all world trade.
Comparative Advantage (David Ricardo, 1817): All countries benefit from cooperation and free trade. A country's ability to produce goods at a lower cost than others. Source: Principles of Political Economy and Taxation.
Key Conclusion: The goal of business = higher profit. The goal of government = protect its people. Neither unrestricted free trade nor total protectionism will accomplish both.
Topic 3

Market Integration

Economic Revolutions

🌾 Agricultural Revolution
  • First major economic change (Pomeranz, 2000)
  • Domesticated plants & animals — more productive than hunter-gatherer
  • Farming created surpluses
  • Led to: permanent settlements, trade networks, population growth
⚙️ Industrial Revolution (1800s)
  • Second major economic revolution
  • New tools: steam engines, manufacturing, mass production
  • Factories changed how work functioned
  • Start of wage labor and skilled workers

Economic Casualties

19th Century Issues: Factory workers (mainly poor women and children) worked in dangerous conditions for low wages. "Barons" (industrialists) gained greater wealth, but also greater economic inequality.
✊
Labor Unions

Organizations of workers that improved wages and working conditions through collective action, strikes, and negotiations. Inspired by Marxist principles. Led to: minimum wage laws, reasonable working hours, safety regulations.

Economic Models

🏦 Capitalism
  • Natural resources & means of production are privately owned
  • Profit maximization & competition as key drivers
  • Incentivized by improving quality & reducing prices
🏛️ Socialism
  • Means of production under collective ownership
  • Property owned by government, allocated to citizens
  • Higher value on meeting everyone's basic needs over individual profit

Information Revolution

Technology has reduced the role of human labor, shifting from a manufacturing-based economy. Computers and technologies are replacing many jobs through automation or outsourcing jobs offshore.

Global Interstate System

Origin of Nation-State: Traced to the Peace of Westphalia — established the notion that nation-states are autonomous.

With globalization, nation-states face challenges: loss of control over economic flows, transnational organizations, and sovereignty. Although the nation-state's role has declined, it remains an important political structure.

3 Forms of Global/Transnational Governance Memorize

  • 1
    Multi-Stakeholder Initiatives
    Brings together public and private actors into policy networks. Involves at least one state actor, international institutions, INGOs, and private organizations. E.g., Global Fund to Fight AIDS, Tuberculosis, and Malaria.
  • 2
    Voluntary Regulations
    MNCs agree to social/environmental practices beyond stated laws. Standards are developed, monitored, and enforced through international organizations.
  • 3
    Courts and Lawyers (Non-International Law)
    Authority not based in international law. Independent investigations can create factual records that pressure states to adhere to their national laws.
Governance Definition (Hale and Held 2011): "Formal and informal rules and institutions that elicit compliance toward collective goals whereby rules are created, compliance is elicited, and goods are provided in pursuit of collective goals."

Essay Preparation

Essay Guide

Use these structured guides to organize your essay responses. Remember: Introduction → Body (arguments + examples) → Conclusion.

"Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of economic globalization, and explain how it affects developing countries."
  • Define economic globalization (IMF 2008 or Szentes 2003 definition)
  • Pros: reduces extreme poverty (UN data — 1.9B to 836M), greater access to goods, growth of TNCs
  • Cons: increases dependency of periphery on core (Wallerstein), loss of national government power, digital era issues
  • Use examples: Coca-Cola/Nike as global brands, WTO free trade, MDGs progress
  • Conclude: Globalization is a double-edged sword — benefits are unequal depending on a country's position (core vs periphery)
"Compare and contrast protectionism and free trade. Which is more beneficial for a developing country?"
  • Define protectionism (McAleese, 2007) and free trade/trade liberalization
  • Protectionism tools: tariffs, quotas, subsidies, standardization — explain each briefly
  • Free trade: GATT (1948) → WTO (1995); comparative advantage (David Ricardo, 1817)
  • For developing nations: free trade opens markets but may hurt local industries; protectionism shields domestic producers but can slow growth
  • Conclude: "Neither unrestricted free trade nor total protectionism will accomplish both goals (profit + people's protection)"
"Explain Wallerstein's World-System Theory and evaluate its relevance to understanding global inequality today."
  • Introduce the theory: Core, Semi-Periphery, Periphery
  • Core: dominates, exploits labor & raw materials (Western Europe, N. America, Japan)
  • Periphery: dependent on core for capital, historically colonized (Africa, parts of Asia, Middle East)
  • Semi-Periphery: middle ground, less dependent (India, Brazil)
  • Relevance: political changes deepened, peripheral nations became more economically dependent; growth of multinational brands reinforces inequalities
  • Conclude: The theory remains relevant but is a simplification of a complex global system
"How did the Agricultural and Industrial Revolutions lay the foundation for market integration and economic globalization?"
  • Agricultural Revolution (Pomeranz, 2000): domestication, surplus farming, trade networks, permanent settlements
  • Industrial Revolution (1800s): steam engines, mass production, wage labor, factory system
  • Economic casualties: inequality, poor working conditions → led to labor unions
  • Labor unions (Marxist inspiration) → minimum wage, reasonable hours, safety
  • Information Revolution: automation, outsourcing → shifts manufacturing-based to knowledge economy
  • Conclude: Each revolution expanded the scope of economic activity and set the stage for modern globalization
✍️ Essay Writing Tips:
• Always define key terms at the start
• Use scholar names and dates as evidence (e.g., "According to Wallerstein…")
• Reference real institutions: UN, WTO, IMF, World Bank
• Use Philippine context when relevant (poverty threshold, ASEAN)
• End with a clear, reasoned conclusion — don't just summarize
