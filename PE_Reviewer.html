Lesson 1

Physical Fitness & Its Components

Definition of Physical Fitness

ACSM Definition: The ability to perform moderate to vigorous levels of physical activity without undue fatigue and the capability of maintaining such ability throughout life.
Physical fitness is an individual condition that varies from person to person. It is influenced by: age, gender, heredity, personal health habits, amount and level of exercise, and eating practices.

2 Components of Physical Fitness

Health-Related Fitness
  • Body Composition
  • Cardiorespiratory Endurance
  • Flexibility
  • Muscular Endurance
  • Muscular Strength
Skill-Related Fitness
  • Agility
  • Balance
  • Coordination
  • Power
  • Reaction Time
  • Speed

5 Benefits of Physical Fitness

  • 1Reduces risk of stress and depression — promotes mental health
  • 2Weight management
  • 3Boosts overall health
  • 4Increases strength and stamina for more rigorous activity
  • 5Reduces risks of diseases and illnesses

General Principles of Fitness Training

Principle of Individualization
Training should be tailored to the individual's specific needs and abilities.
Principle of Specificity
Training should be specific to the goals and activities the person wants to improve in.
Principle of Overload — FITT
F — Frequency: increasing the number of times you train per week
I — Intensity: increasing the difficulty of the exercise (e.g., running 12 km/h instead of 10)
T — Type: the kind of exercises a person does
T — Time: increasing the length of each training session (e.g., cycling 45 min instead of 30)
Lesson 2

Safety in Physical Activity

Pre-Exercise Screening

Pre-exercise screening serves as a checkpoint or "safety net" to help you determine if the possible advantages of exercise outweigh the risks. Read the pre-exercise self-screening tool before beginning any program.

Safety Rules in Physical Activity

  • 1Consider the technique used and your individual condition (injury history, fitness level)
  • 2Exercises that cause pain or discomfort should be avoided or modified
  • 3Do not ignore signs of exhaustion, discomfort, and pain
  • 4Injuries require rest — "pushing through" pain damages delicate muscle tissue and delays healing
  • 5Have at least one recovery day, preferably two, every week

Safety Concerns During Exercise

  • 1Exercise in a secure location and at a secure time of day
  • 2Dress appropriately — light colors or reflective coverings, loose-fitting clothing
  • 3Wear shoes that fit properly and are appropriate for the activity
  • 4Always warm up before and cool down after exercise to reduce risk of strains and sprains
  • 5Take appropriate breaks during activity
  • 6Do not exercise on an empty stomach — eat something light (e.g., toast with jam, skimmed milk)
  • 7Do not exercise immediately after a full meal — it affects digestion
  • 8Listen to your body — stop if you feel dizziness, shortness of breath, chest pain, nausea, or muscle/joint pain

Types of Diet

Lesson 3

Warm-Up & Cool Down

What is a Warm-Up?

A warm-up consists of different gentle exercises that gradually increase in intensity — including stretching and simple moderate-intensity exercises that cause sweating and gradually increase muscle temperature. It helps prevent injuries and accidents while doing exercise.

7 Benefits of Warm-Up Exercises

  • 1Increases blood flow to muscles — enhances delivery of oxygen and nutrients
  • 2Warms muscles — promotes energy-releasing reactions and makes muscles more supple
  • 3Prepares muscles for more tedious stretching
  • 4Prepares the heart for an increase in activity
  • 5Prepares mental setup for upcoming heavier exercises
  • 6Enhances ability to learn and perform skilled movements
  • 7Reduces risk of injury in joints, muscles, and tendons

What is Cool Down?

Also called warming down. It is the gradual conversion of the intensity of exercise from hard → moderate → easy, allowing gradual decrease in the body's exerted energy until it reaches the resting or near-resting state.

5 Benefits of Cool Down

  • 1Allows heart rate to return to its normal resting state
  • 2Helps avoid fainting or dizziness
  • 3Helps prepare muscles for the next time you exercise
  • 4Prevents Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness (DOMS)
  • 5Prevents blood pooling

Designing a Fitness Program

How to Design an Effective Fitness Program

  • 1Environmental factors
  • 2Need analysis
  • 3Program Variables / Training Guidelines

6 Types of Training Exercises

Continuous Training
Any form of training that maintains the heart rate at a desired level over a sustained period of time.
Circuit Training
Involves exercises at a variety of different stations. Example: 6 stations with 30 seconds of activity at each.
Weight Training
A method of training using weights.
Interval Training
Exercising with periods of rest planned into the session.
Plyometric Training
Involves exercises with short bursts of high intensity.
Flexibility Training
Completing certain exercises that will improve a person's range of motion around a joint.

Training Techniques — DO's and DON'Ts

✅ DO's
  • Lift an appropriate amount of weight
  • Use proper form
  • Breathe and rest
  • Seek balance
  • Add strength training in your routine
❌ DON'Ts
  • Don't skip the warm-up
  • Don't rush
  • Don't overdo
  • Don't ignore pain
  • Don't forget your shoes
